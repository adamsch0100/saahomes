National housing market · September 2026 release

The U.S. housing market in September 2026 carried more supply than it has in years at the same time the two halves of it moved in different directions. Existing-home sales ran at a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 3.99 million, up 1.5% from August, and 1.60 million homes were for sale — 4.8 months of supply, unchanged from August and up from 4.5 a year earlier. The median existing-home price was $417,500, up 1.3% year over year, the 39th straight month of annual gains, and the typical home sat 33 days on the market. The average 30-year fixed mortgage rate was 6.86% in September, up from 6.35% a year earlier (Freddie Mac). New single-family home sales, the most recent national release, ran at 684,000 in August with 8.5 months of new-home supply. Both can be true at once: buyers have more to choose from, and sellers who price to the current set still get paid.

How to use these numbers in a listing appointment

A national number is context, not a price. The seller's question is what their home will sell for against the homes a buyer would cross-shop. Put the two together: 4.8 months of existing-home supply tells a seller the market has choice, and the local competitive set tells them which choice a buyer makes. FAQ

How much housing supply is there in the U.S. right now?

As of September 2026 there were 4.8 months of existing-home supply — unchanged from August and up from 4.5 months a year earlier — with 1.60 million existing homes for sale (National Association of REALTORS®). New-home supply stood at 8.5 months at the August sales pace (U.S. Census Bureau/HUD, via FRED).

Are home prices rising or falling?

They are moving in opposite directions by segment. The median existing-home price was $417,500 in September 2026, up 1.3% year over year — the 39th straight month of annual gains (NAR). The median new-home sale price was $393,700 in August 2026, down 5.8% year over year (Census/HUD).
