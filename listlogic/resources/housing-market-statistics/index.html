National housing market · August 2026

The U.S. housing market in August 2026 carried more supply than it has in years at the same time the two halves of it moved in different directions. New single-family home sales ran at a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 684,000, up 6.4% from July, with 8.5 months of new-home supply on the market. Existing-home sales ran below 4 million for the first time since June 2025, while the median existing-home price rose for the 38th straight month. Both can be true at once: buyers have more to choose from, and sellers who price to the current set still get paid.

How to use these numbers in a listing appointment

A national number is context, not a price. The seller's question is what their home will sell for against the homes a buyer would cross-shop. Put the two together: 4.9 months of existing-home supply tells a seller the market has choice, and the local competitive set tells them which choice a buyer makes. FAQ

How much housing supply is there in the U.S. right now?

As of August 2026 there were 8.5 months of new-home supply at the current sales pace (U.S. Census Bureau/HUD, via FRED) and 4.9 months of existing-home supply, up from 4.6 in July (National Association of REALTORS®).

Are home prices rising or falling?

They are moving in opposite directions by segment. The median new-home sale price was $393,700 in August 2026, down 5.8% year over year (Census/HUD), while the median existing-home price was $429,100, up 1.6% year over year — the 38th straight month of annual gains (NAR).
