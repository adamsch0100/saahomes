The listing appointment checklist

What to bring to a listing appointment: condition-adjusted comps, a price × odds × supply story, and a print leave-behind the seller can defend after you leave.

The listing appointment is not won at the table. It is won the night before, when you decide what you will put in front of the seller.

Charm books the meeting. Preparation is what they remember when the other agent promised more. When it is hard to sell, sellers want the expert who can prove the price. That proof is not a branded folder of marketing pages. It is a condition-adjusted competitive set, a price × odds × supply story they can move, and a leave-behind of the same story.

Most listing presentation checklists stop at the classic packet: a CMA printout, the company brochure, testimonials, staging tips, and a recommended price. That kit is fine for a tour. It does not survive the spouse, the friend who “knows real estate,” or the agent who walked in with a higher number.

This listing appointment checklist is the bag you carry into the appointment that does. How to win listing appointments is the four-part method. This is listing appointment preparation for that method — research, the pricing story, proof materials, the conversation, and the close. Skim the lists. Pack the proof items, not just the marketing.

1. Research — comps, condition, and supply

Listing appointment preparation starts in the pull, not the pitch. If you cherry-pick six solds that support your number, the next agent — or the seller’s friend — will find the seventh.

  • Pull the field a buyer will actually cross-shop. Last two years; matching property type and area; square footage, beds, baths, garage, and lot around the subject.
  • Include Active, Pending or Backup, Sold, Expired, and Withdrawn. Expireds and withdrawns are the supply story sellers forget.
  • Note days on market on every card. DOM is how you talk about pace without inventing a statistic.
  • Flag condition on every comparable. Kitchen, baths, garage, lot, updates. Two similar cards are not twins.
  • Put the high sold that is not comparable in the set and write why. Hiding it does not make it disappear.
  • Know how you got the data. Search any market nationwide, or upload any MLS export — CSV or TXT from Matrix, Flexmls, Bright, or a RESO-style file. You do not need an MLS data service to prepare.

Illustrative — not a measured result: a seller is anchored $30,000 above matching solds because a renovated home three streets over “got that.” You already have that home in the set, with kitchen, lot, and list-to-close path noted. The stretch stops being a hope before you sit down.

2. The pricing story — the curve and the odds

A packet of comps still lets a seller say “let’s try my number for 30 days.” The curve is what ends that sentence. This is the listing presentation checklist item most packets skip.

  • Build three list prices: matching, stretch, and conservative. Not one recommended number you still have to argue.
  • Price — the number on the sign, at each of those three points.
  • Odds — a modeled likelihood of a clean sale in a realistic window, typically something like 60–90 days. A trade-off, not a guarantee.
  • Supply — how many homes a buyer would genuinely cross-shop at that price. This field. Not city-wide months of inventory.
  • Be ready to move the list price in the room. An interactive CMA is not a PDF with a slider screenshot.
  • Write the league change in one sentence. “Stretch $X and the home sits next to these nicer listings.”

Move the list price up and the field changes. The home stops competing with similar listings and starts sitting next to nicer ones. The odds drop because the buyer pool shrinks. You do not have to say “your house is overpriced.” They can see the league change. That is also how to price a listing in a soft market — on a clock, with the field in view.

Illustrative — not a measured result: at the matching price, two similar actives sit next to the home. Stretch $20,000 and eight nicer listings enter the field. The seller asked for the higher number. Now they can see what it costs.

3. Proof materials — the leave-behind

You do not win the listing only at the table. You win it in the group chat after you leave. The spouse who was at work, the friend who “knows real estate,” the other agent’s packet — they will reopen the number. If they only have your opinion, the higher promise wins.

  • Print the same story you will walk — the condition-adjusted set plus the price × odds × supply curve. Not a different branded PDF with a frozen recommended price.
  • Send the flipbook. The person who was not in the room should see what you showed, not a brochure.
  • One-page summary: three prices, odds at each, competing supply at each. They will screenshot this.
  • Notes that justify the condition adjustments you will say out loud — kitchen, garage, lot, updates.
  • Your contact and the next-step card. Marketing materials can ride along. They are not the proof.

The leave-behind is not a souvenir. It is how you stay in the room after you leave. If the interactive version and the packet tell different stories, the group chat rewrites the appointment.

4. The conversation — listen, then walk the curve

What to bring to a listing appointment includes a sequence, not a monologue. Rapport gets you invited back. Proof is why they sign with you instead of the agent who promised more.

  • Listen first. Tour. Ask timeline, proceeds, what they have already heard. The portal estimate and the neighbor story are already in the room. Name them.
  • Do not open with your number or theirs. Lead with the field a buyer will cross-shop. A number without a field is an opinion.
  • Anchor on the competitive set, then walk the curve. Land a recommendation only after they can see the trade-off.
  • Walk condition out loud. “Same beds and baths. Their kitchen was done last year. Yours is original. That is why they sit in a different band.”
  • When they ask for the stretch, move the price. Show odds and supply at their number. Make the stretch a visible choice, not a promise.

If the number comes back later, you already have the field. See how to handle the price objection without turning the hour into a debate about who is right.

5. Close — next steps and follow-up

A listing appointment checklist that ends at “ask for the listing” leaves the night unfinished. The close is the same story, written down, sent the same evening.

  • Agree the next step in the room: sign, sleep on it, or send the flipbook to the person who was not there.
  • If they want the stretch, write it down as their choice, with the odds they saw. Or decide the listing is not one you will take.
  • Send the matching print or flipbook the same evening. Same story — not a recap email that rewrites the number.
  • Follow up on the decision, not a new price. If they come back with the other agent’s higher promise, walk the field again.
  • Calendar the first pricing check if they list. The curve is useful after the sign goes up, not only before. The soft-market listing presentation does not retire at the kitchen table.

What not to bring

The listing presentation checklist is also a leave-out list. Unproofed numbers lose the appointment — or win you a listing you will regret taking.

Leave this out Why it fails Bring this instead
A recommended price with no field An opinion. Loses to the higher opinion. Three prices with odds and supply at each
Cherry-picked solds The seventh comparable shows up tomorrow The honest set, including the high sold you explain
A portal printout you plan to argue Sounds like you are talking them down The estimate next to this field
Marketing packet, no matching curve They only remember the number they wanted Print + flipbook of the same story
Stats you cannot source Win rates and “priced right” slogans DOM and supply on this field, this week

Do not bring a different story in print than the one you walked on screen. If you cannot defend a number after you leave, do not put it on the table.

What belongs on a listing appointment checklist?

A listing appointment checklist should cover research (condition-adjusted comps, supply, days on market), the pricing story (interactive price × odds × supply), proof materials (print or flipbook leave-behind), the conversation (listen first, then walk the curve), and the close (next steps and same-night follow-up). The proof items are what win the appointment — not just the marketing packet.

What should I bring to a listing appointment?

Bring a condition-adjusted competitive set, a price × odds × supply curve the seller can move, and a leave-behind of the same story. Brochures and testimonials can ride along. They do not replace the proof kit.
