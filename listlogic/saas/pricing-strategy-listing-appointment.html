The listing appointment is a pricing decision, not a report handoff.

Sellers already have a number — from a portal, a neighbor, the last agent who promised more. The agent who wins the listing is the one who can show what happens if they stretch the price: how odds and timing change against real supply. That is the pricing strategy conversation.

The three jobs of a pricing strategy appointment

A static CMA shows comps and maybe a suggested list price. A pricing strategy appointment makes the seller a participant in the decision — so they own the number and the plan.

Job 1

Build the custom-fit competitive set

City-wide averages lie. Filter to the homes a buyer would genuinely cross-shop against the subject — size, garage, area, age, timeframe — so every number reflects this home's competitive set. Apples-to-apples is what makes the pricing factual.

Job 2

Walk the trade-off curve

Move the list price and show how modeled odds and supply pressure change together. Stretch the number and competing supply rises while odds drop. The seller sees the league change — you do not have to say "overpriced."

Job 3

Leave proof that survives the group chat

Print the same story or send the flipbook. The spouse and the friend who "knows real estate" reopen the field, not just your opinion. A brochure and a recommended price is an opinion; a field they can move is proof.

Illustrative — not a measured result

Same week, two appointments

One agent leaves a 20-page solds packet and a promised number. The other walks the curve, names the renovated sold that is not comparable, and leaves the flipbook. The seller hires the proof, not the page count.

Fair questions, honest answers

What is a pricing strategy listing appointment?

A pricing strategy listing appointment is the meeting where the agent and seller agree on a list price and a plan — before the sign goes up. The agent who wins it shows the seller a custom-fit competitive set, a recommended range, and the trade-offs at each price point: what happens to odds and timing if they stretch. It is a pricing decision, not a report handoff.

How do I talk about price with a seller who is anchored on Zillow?

Do not argue the portal number. Show the seller their own competitive set — the homes a buyer would actually cross-shop — and walk the price-versus-odds curve. When the seller sees that stretching the price moves them into a league with more supply and lower modeled odds, the conversation shifts from opinion to trade-offs. The portal estimate can appear as an optional chip so you can talk about the number they already saw.

What should I bring to a listing appointment to prove the price?

Bring a custom-fit competitive set (not city-wide averages), a recommended list price and range, and an interactive way to show trade-offs — live what-if sliders that move price and show how odds and supply change. Leave the same story as a print leave-behind or flipbook so the seller can defend it after you leave. A static CMA report is an opinion; a field they can move is proof.
