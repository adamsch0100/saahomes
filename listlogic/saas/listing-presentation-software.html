How a listing presentation should win — the proof-kit framework

A digital listing presentation that only recites your bio loses to the agent who promised a higher number. Win the listing by replacing argument with a field the seller can see.

Step 1

Be the expert who can prove the price

When the market is soft, the seller is not shopping for the prettiest deck. They are shopping for the agent who can defend a number after you leave — against Zillow, the neighbor's story, and the other agent's stretch promise.

Step 2

Show the condition-adjusted set

Not a zip-code median. The homes a buyer will actually cross-shop — type, area, size, beds, baths, garage, lot — then adjusted for condition. That is the field. A stretch price that sits in a nicer league is visible, not argued.

Step 3

Walk price × odds × supply

For each list price, show the number on the sign, the modeled odds of a clean sale in a realistic window, and how many homes a buyer would cross-shop at that price. Move the slider. The objection becomes a choice.

Step 4

Leave the same story behind

Print it. Send the flipbook. After you walk out, the spouse and the friend who “knows real estate” reopen the conversation. If they only have your opinion, the higher number wins the group chat.

Step 5 — after the sign goes up

Keep proving it with the Market Fingerprint

The listing is won, but the conversation does not end. The Market Fingerprint is a living seller link that tracks the same competitive set weekly — what listed under them, what went pending, where they sit — until the home sells. Your note when you have a take, never an auto-generated price cut.

Illustrative, not a measured result: two agents present the same week. One leaves a branded 28-page PDF. The other walks condition-adjusted comps and shows odds drop as supply widens $20,000 above the set — then leaves that curve on the table. The seller hires the proof, not the page count.
