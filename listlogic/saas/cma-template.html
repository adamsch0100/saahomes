The winning CMA structure — use this as your template

This is the free CMA template: five sections a seller-facing comparative market analysis should contain. Not a downloadable blank PDF. Fill it from the seller's market — or generate it in ListLogic.

Section 1

The subject

Address, type, beds, baths, sq ft, garage, lot, year, and condition. Condition decides whether two same-size houses are the same listing. Write what the seller believes it is worth — and what a buyer will actually cross-shop against it.

Section 2

Condition-adjusted comps

Not the last five solds in the zip. The set a buyer will tour: matching type and area; size, beds, baths, garage, lot around the subject; Active, Pending/Backup, Sold, Expired, and Withdrawn — then adjust for condition.

Section 3

The price curve and odds

A number in a box is not a CMA. Show what happens to modeled odds of a clean sale in a realistic window if you list with the matching solds — and if you stretch. That curve is the conversation.

Section 4

Competing supply

At each list price, which homes enter the buyer's set? Stretch the number and you often compete with nicer inventory. Sellers argue price; they understand a league change.

Section 5

The recommendation / leave-behind

One decision, not a 28-page packet. The same story you walked: the set, the curve, the supply. Leave it as print and a flipbook the spouse who was not in the room can reopen.
